# Grandado Ø90/86 cylindrical mounting kit

This kit combines an internal cylindrical cassette, two solid end discs, a smooth curved replacement Grandado cover and a segmented marking jig. It uses the existing library Grandado 83 × 58 × 35 mm body and BeiLaMoo 3S 18650 wire-lead holder. The holder body is 77.7 × 59.69 × 21.54 mm. Three library cell envelopes are Ø18.6 × 65.2 mm; these references do not specify an electrical battery configuration.

## Parts and hardware

The internal core has four printed pieces: one upper shell, two lower axial halves meeting at X 0, and one full passive battery keeper. The lower halves include the holder floor. The keeper captures the cell roofs and upper end rims through broad keyed rails. Two 16 mm solid end discs capture the core halves and keeper tails through integral keys and shoulders.

Each disc uses four radial anchors through the pipe: **eight DIN 7991 M3 ×12 screws and eight M3 hex nuts** in total. There are no added internal screws or antenna-cover anchors. The four Grandado lid screws belong to the existing purchased enclosure; their modified installation is described below. Set the factory flat lid aside for this installation.

The fitting kit has 18 printed components: four core pieces, two end discs, one cover and 11 jig pieces. The fit coupon brings the fitting export to 19. A separate nominal-window jig has 11 alternative components; print one jig variant. No antenna cover is included.

## Design decision and load path

The requested final functions are a smooth curved lid, preserved confirmed four-hole pattern, a recessed gasket channel, body-rim contact and the original eight pipe anchors. The antenna cover is excluded from this kit and preserved separately as `jcg108-tube-fairing`; this kit retains its original eight M3 ×12 pipe anchors. Existing Grandado and BeiLaMoo library identities are reused. The Grandado source geometry is corrected using the new delivered-sample measurements, while its SKU and published outside envelope remain unchanged.

The ideal final result places reinforcement inside the available lid space while leaving the outside cylindrical. The contradiction is that raised reinforcement improves head support but interrupts the smooth exterior. Separation in space moves the screw load columns inward; lowering the bearing faces retains a 3 mm web, while shortening the existing screw tips avoids blind-hole bottoming. Available resources are the existing body posts/inserts, four proven axes, original screws and the unused depth under the roof. No numbered matrix mapping is claimed. Method references: [TRIZ Body of Knowledge](https://www.aitriz.org/triz/triz-body-of-knowledge) and [MATRIZ terminology](https://wiki.matriz.org/docs/triz/glossary-6146/).

Retaining the previous raised bosses fails the smooth-surface requirement. Simply clipping those bosses with unchanged screw seats leaves protruding heads and inadequate local seating stock. A new screw SKU would require an unverified thread identity. The selected internal-column concept preserves the original thread and removes 2 mm only from each original screw tip. Its tradeoff is a delivered-part depth/length check and tip-finishing operation. Loads pass from each screw head through the internal bearing web to its existing body post and insert; the gasket occupies its recessed channel and the body seating faces control closure. The groove does not replace screw clamping. No physical preload, impact strength or leakage test is claimed.

The measured channel creates a second contradiction: making its floor uniformly 2 mm deep would locally consume the 3 mm radial roof while increasing the outer roof would violate the fixed 1 mm apex projection. The selected separation in space clips only the outer edge of the cutter to the existing R43 inner roof, retaining the roof stock in smaller-pipe configurations. After the delivered-rim correction, the default Ø90 channel now has a full 2 mm floor. It retains the channel and inner-wall widths, the smooth outside cylinder and the load-bearing stock. A dry tongue was rejected because it did not retain a gasket in a lid-side channel; the former narrow round-cord prototype was replaced when the user supplied the actual section. The sealing stiles are deliberate interface features, separate from the screw-bearing load webs.

The cable correction adds a third design contradiction: a permanent cable crosses the path of a sliding load-bearing end. The ideal result is for the existing sections to close over that cable without moving it or adding fasteners. The selected spatial separation trims the closing edge over its entire required axial sweep, using free space above and inboard of the torque lands. Static final-state holes were inadequate. A late lateral lay-in path was considered, but a simple sideways movement of the descending leg would hit a torque land; that unverified feed sequence was rejected. The chosen sequence routes the cable in the accessible upper assembly first and then closes the lower group along the cleared axis. The local loading opening and integral floor ties retain assembly access and connectivity without extra parts.

## Assembly order

1. Measure the tube, Grandado body/ears, holder, cells, connectors and screw heads. Print the coupon and verify the selected fits.
2. Mark and prepare the window, eight radial anchor holes and optional antenna hole using the assembled jig. Deburr and insulate edges where cables pass.
3. Outside the tube, load the holder and fit the full passive keeper over the cells. Check the roof, rim-stop and wire clearances.
4. Slide the two lower axial halves onto this loaded holder/keeper assembly from opposite ends until they meet at X 0.
5. Feed the attached lead outward through the upper loading opening while lifting Grandado and its original inserts into the upper shell. With the lower group and discs still absent, place the wire in the side transfer and underfloor route; feed its free end into the open negative-end entry of the lower assembly. Slide that loaded group from X+ along the keyed tracks over the fixed routed wire. Do not leave slack across an unrelieved joint.
6. Check the routed wire through the open underside. Load four M3 nuts into each end disc and lay end-crossing leads into the open lower grooves before fitting the discs. Seat the disc keys and shoulders around the lower halves and keeper tails, checking each joint without pulling the cable.
7. Slide the complete bare-body cassette and discs axially into the open tube at the final height. Align the disc anchor holes with the eight marked pipe holes.
8. Install all eight M3 ×12 radial anchors. Route the antenna lead through its prepared entry and install the antenna directly on the tube without a cover. Check the actual plug and strain relief.
9. Clean the lid's gasket channel, underside seating faces and four head recesses. Measure the supplied lid screws and blind-hole depths. Shorten the original screw tips by the verified 2 mm allowance, deburr and confirm that the heads clamp the 3 mm webs without bottoming.
10. Complete strain-free power/SMA routing. Place the correctly measured gasket in the lid channel, fit the smooth lid from above, and tighten the four original-thread screws evenly. Confirm the body rim compresses the gasket while the intended hard seating faces stop closure; do not trap the gasket across a screw passage.


For service, disconnect external wiring and remove the cover and its screws before withdrawing the cassette axially. Release the radial anchors and service the loaded core outside the tube, where the disc keys and keeper are accessible.
