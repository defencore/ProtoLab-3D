# Rocket Release Mechanism

The original four-gearmotor and MG90S layouts and their eight tube-size presets are preserved. The default is the original four-motor Ø80/76 assembly, without batteries or parachute brackets. ST3215 is a separate layout with its own Ø90/86 preset; selecting it from a smaller tube raises the dimensions to 90/86.

## ST3215 nose release

The nose layout is inverted. Its servo, mounting disk, gear train and spring cartridges belong to the nose and leave with it. The one-piece locking ring with its 30 mm tall, 3 mm wall rim remains in the main body. Two rows of four radial screws attach this machined part directly to the tube; no axial ring-to-rim screws are used. During the first 60% of the position sequence the nose turns through the unlocking angle; afterwards it translates toward +Z. This is relative mechanism motion, not an ejection-time or force simulation. The parachute attachment and extraction cord are not included.

ST3215 is clamped against the 2 mm nose disk by four Ø4 metal standoffs with M2 end threads and one machined rear plate with 2 mm webs, lightening openings and stepped contact pads from 3.5 mm stock. Four Ø3.4×3.1 front support pillars have Ø1.5 locating pins fitting the stock front pilot holes and matching disk holes. The case is retained by compression through the metal structure; no mounting threads are added to the plastic servo case. The nose disk has four integral curved tube-mounting walls (10 mm high, approximately 3 mm thick), gear axle seats, spring-barrel bosses and four posts for the gear retaining disk. There is no output coupling, stock output disc or gear shield.

The one-piece Al7075 input pinion has 20 involute teeth and an integral Ø8 hub with a 25-tooth spline copied from a section of the supplier shaft. The spline cavity has 0.04 mm radial clearance. A recessed centre fastener retains the gear, so no separate coupler is used. The supplier CAD bore has a nonstandard modeled thread: the retaining screw is explicitly a CAD-fit reference, derived from that bore, with an assumed Ø5×1 low head. Confirm the actual supplied retaining screw and fit before manufacturing; no standard thread designation is inferred from this CAD representation.

Four POM idlers with plain Ø3.1 bores turn on captive Ø3 steel axles. The axles locate in blind Ø3.04 seats in the nose disk and a common 2 mm Al6061 retaining disk. Four M2×6 90° countersunk screws secure that disk to posts machined into the nose disk. The retaining disk has a central clearance hole for the input pinion; its outer radius stays 0.4 mm inside the internal gear tooth tips so it can pass through the body ring during separation. The gears have 0.15 mm axial clearance on each side. There are no individual axle-end screws, D-shaped idler bores or separate axle bushings.

Each M8×0.75 spring barrel screws 3.5 mm into the nose disk and its integral boss. The Ø10×1.2 flange bears on the disk behind the Ø8 threaded opening, so the barrel cannot pass through it. The gear-side mouth is open Ø6.4 with an integral Ø4 throat. Air can pass around the Ø2 rod and Ø3.2 button neck. The Ø5.8 piston shoulders against that throat at full travel; the retaining ring is a backup with 0.2 mm remaining clearance to the rear plug. The piston is installed through the open rear before fitting the removable M7×0.5 guide plug. A separate steel M2-threaded contact button is fitted from the front and requires threadlocking. The external split retaining ring remains in the rod groove.

Both tubes have the same outside diameter and a configurable axial seam, initially 0.4 mm. The ST3215 presets use 0.25 mm radial installation clearance; the original presets retain 0.5 mm. Nose and body radial screws have 90° countersunk Ø4.4×1.2 heads, recessed 0.08 mm below the local tangent plane so their edges stay within the cylindrical outer surface. These are nominal CAD fits, not tolerance-stack or aerodynamic validation. Check actual tube ovality, machining tolerance and coatings before choosing the final seam.

The spring-sizing controls are preliminary: six active turns, mean Ø5 mm, adjustable wire (initial Ø0.7), free length equal to 9.2 mm installed length + stroke + remaining preload (initial 2 mm). Rate uses `G*d^4/(8*D^3*n)` with assumed spring-steel `G=79,000 N/mm²`. Four spring forces and released energy are compared with measured extraction/friction resistance, upward gravity and target kinetic energy over the full extraction distance, with a 1.5× energy allowance. Initial inputs (0.30 kg, 5 N, 30 mm, 1 m/s) are assumptions. The panel reports estimated Wahl-corrected wire stress; no allowable stress, spring supplier rating, impact load or attachment strength is certified. The geometric spring has uniform pitch; actual end treatment and rated load require a selected supplier spring and a bench extraction test. See [Lee Spring design considerations](https://www.leespring.com/learn-about-compression-springs).

The powered ST3215 preset uses the 3S1P pack and HAT described below; the compact unpowered preset remains separate. The original four-motor and MG90S defaults remain without batteries.

## CAD

Six installed ST3215 components retain the supplier CAD geometry, source SHA-256 `58e38e4dc49f97df738c5f229f9aa8a7dce64a0a1d01335486a52d53e6017e8a`. The MG90S and cylindrical gearmotors in the original layouts remain installation envelopes. The new layout has modeled fastener threads, mating threaded holes, 20° involute gear profiles, and separate FreeCAD labels with model, material, dimensions and manufacturing identifiers. Existing original variants keep their original geometric recipes.

`tests/release-mechanism.test.ts` checks original defaults, drive kinematics, preset isolation and which parts leave with the nose. `scripts/verify-recovery.ts` prepares preview and native geometry; `scripts/verify-recovery.py` checks closed solids, metrology, interference and FCStd roundtrips. Supplier-internal overlaps are compared with the source geometry. STEP conversion is an optional separate check; it is not established for the rotated supplier output shaft.

## Powered ST3215 nose: 3S1P and Waveshare HAT (A)

The powered Ø90/86 preset uses three 18650 cells in series (not parallel). Cell 2 is reversed; two formed nickel reference tabs join cell 1+ → cell 2− and cell 2+ → cell 3−. B− and B+ remain the pack output. Final welds, insulated flexible wiring, selected 3S protection/balance board, fuse, connector plugs and strain relief must be detailed for the selected cells; no unspecified BMS is represented as a purchased component.

The cells now sit in **two common insulating plates**, machined POM by default or selectable printed PA12 prototypes rather than individual cups, lids and nine thin cage ties. Each plate has three Ø19 mm seats with 6 mm lateral engagement and Ø12 terminal access. The outer plate is backed by the 3 mm Al6061 structural frame. Through-pockets remove material outside the cell seats, tie bosses and wire passages while retaining at least 3 mm webs to those interfaces; the HAT mounting rails remain supported. The servo-side plate has a common 2 mm Al6061 backing plate; openings clear the servo, its clamps and all four spring cartridges. Four outer flats leave at least 0.5 mm clearance from the integral nose mounting walls while retaining the diagonal tie shoulders. Four stepped Ø5/Ø3 metal ties and four short clamping sleeves capture both plates and attach the pack to the same nose disk as the ST3215. The POM separates the cells from metal. The nominal cell-to-seat radial clearance is 0.2 mm; verify the actual cell jacket, end shape and machining tolerances. Upper silicone end cushions remain. The PA12 option preserves the geometry and metal backing, and is marked PRINT in FreeCAD labels. A selected insulating, unfilled PA12 grade and print process must be qualified for the temperatures and loads; carbon-filled/ESD material is not an equivalent insulation substitute. [Formlabs SLS electrical data](https://formlabs.com/eu/support/Electrical-properties-of-selected-Formlabs-SLS-powders/) identifies its unfilled Nylon 12 as insulating; these material data are not a qualification of this battery assembly. These are structural retention features, not a validated crash rating.

Two dedicated Ø8 holes align through the frame and both common plates. Flanged TPU liners leave Ø6 clear wire passages and shield the edges; these are not cable clamps. The B1 reference bridge is raised above the new backing plate. Harness diameter, routing, strain relief and selected protection electronics remain to be completed with the real wiring.

The HAT carries no battery loads. Its four Ø3 supplier holes receive M2.5 screws, insulating washers and Ø3.5/2.8 steel compression limiters. A **single printed PA12 U-frame cradle** and **1 mm silicone rubber gasket** are clamped against the metal frame by these same four screws; there are no separate M2 support bolts or nuts. Two short contact towers carry Ø5×1 silicone pads beneath verified clear PCB underside regions. The metal sleeves set the stack height to limit compression; the printed cradle clears the underside components, nickel bridge and harness liners. This is a compliant support, not a specified shock isolator. Rubber hardness, print process, creep, vibration and impact performance remain unvalidated. The supplier board's installed radial envelope is 42.121 mm, leaving 0.879 mm to an ideal Ø86 bore. Flexible mating connectors still need space; side-facing sockets are not assumed to accept a straight plug against the tube wall.

The controller faces towards the nose above the cells. Its added compartment extends the tube reference by 25 mm, while the release disk, spring cartridges and gear clearances remain unchanged. The compact unpowered ST3215 layout and the eight original presets remain available; the project default is still the original 80/76 layout without cells.

Supplier STEP is stored privately in `hat-native.json`, matching the independent library package. All 361 source solids are retained, except two invalid small underside ICs use their measured envelopes; a connector receives a volume-preserving topology repair. See asset hash and repair record. Library and assembly FreeCAD exports retain the model name/SKU and native PCB/connector geometry.

Export raises the minimum OpenCascade topology tolerance to 0.00001 mm while retaining larger supplier tolerances. This preserves every coordinate, surface and solid volume, and prevents numerical orientation failures when the board is rotated and saved to FCStd.

The HAT accepts 9–25 V, **matching the servo supply**, rather than regulating the servo output. The standard ST3215 12 V variant accepts 6–12.6 V; a 3S Li-ion pack is 11.1 V nominal, 12.6 V full. The 7.4 V variant is incompatible with this pack. Powered preset nose mass starts at an explicitly assumed 0.50 kg. User-editable HAT mass/CG join the cell/servo balance model; this is not the complete rocket CG. `designG` reports inertial demand, not a strength limit. Mount pull-out, PCB bending, shock, vibration, cell retention and final spring performance still require measured loads and verification.

## Large assembly preview

Recovery geometry, bounds, CAD script and STL preparation run in a cancellable Web Worker. Typed vertex buffers are transferred to the viewer; a parameter change cancels the obsolete worker. Large assemblies omit the costly edge-overlay construction while retaining solid surface detail and the wireframe display mode. This keeps model generation out of the UI thread. Export is enabled only when the current model is ready.

## MG996R / Tattu / WING MINI nose package

Three additional Ø90/86 presets select **TowerPro MG996R**, **Tattu Classic 650 mAh 75C 2S1P or 3S1P**, and **SpeedyBee F405 WING-MINI FC/PDB/shield**. The third preset uses two vertical 18650 cells in 2S1P beside the servo. Existing original and ST3215 configurations are retained. All power hardware travels with the nose.

The transverse battery sits between a metal lower plate and the servo heel plate, carried by four symmetric Al6061 columns at (±31, ±18) mm. Integral metal corner stops locate the liners against lateral movement. Continuous printable PA12 insulating seats, broad silicone pads and side/end stops locate the pouch; no screw threads carry impact load through printed battery tabs. The servo case has a 2 mm heel web with a 4 mm locating rim (6 mm total stock height), four side pads, a heel cushion and a front cushion against an integral mounting-disk support. This gives a case-to-metal load path independent of the plastic ears.

The FC is horizontal. In the 2×18650 layout it mounts directly to the servo heel through four bonded elastomer dampers secured with M2 countersunk screws; there is no separate controller shelf. The LiPo variants retain their perforated Al5052 shelf because the pouch occupies the heel face. Components face toward the nose tip. The sleeve includes 12 mm free axial space above the PLS pins for plugs/wire bends, an exposed BAT/GND pad corridor and 10 mm lateral SH plug insertion space. The former side bracket is removed. Separate shield C, FC B and PDB A models include the supplier's 3 mm and 6.5 mm copper spacers, PWM header pins, major components and connector access. In the LiPo layouts, four shield pads carry mounting force outside the component field. The schematic board outlines and 18×22 mm hole pitch are inferred fit references, **not OEM CAD**; pitch is editable. The 1 mm PCB thickness is an explicit modeling assumption. Confirm actual stack and plugs before manufacture. The installed mass defaults to 15.8 g (19 g kit minus 3.2 g removable wireless extender).

The battery's nominal X offset automatically cancels the electronic payload moment from measured servo and controller masses. `lipoTrimX` supports correction after weighing the complete assembly; the live report clearly excludes structural parts and wiring from this CG estimate. At the configurable acceleration, it reports battery inertia demand, not a certified retention strength. The sleeve now includes the actual controller and mating-connector service space, not just bare PCB dimensions.

Connection schedule: XT30 positive/negative → PDB **BAT/GND**; servo power/ground → **Vx/GND**, servo signal → **S3** configured as a PWM servo output. Keep the servo BEC at its default 5 V. **Vv defaults to battery voltage and is not a servo power source.** On 2S, loaded voltage must remain above the manufacturer's 7 V input minimum: not all nominal battery capacity is usable. The wireless extender is a removable setup accessory, excluded from the installed envelope. The battery power and balance lead envelopes exit the end wall and pass through a lined 12×4 mm clear opening in the heel plate. XT30 and JST-XHR plug envelopes remain accessible in the side corridor; the bend envelopes are installation references. The remaining FC/servo harness, fuse/arming connector and service loops require installation planning.

Pack envelope and mass are editable; selecting 2S/3S restores the corresponding supplier nominal values. Vendor dimensional tolerances and wrapper variation mean the actual pack must be measured. The MG996R page contains differing dimension tables and no spline-flank drawing. Ear pitches (initial 48×10 mm), shaft major/root diameters and screw interface are **prototype fit references**, with matching controls. Do not treat the input pinion as a production-certified servo spline. Validate actual case, ears, retaining screw, board shield contact regions and connector access before machining/printing. The main MG996R body and output are installation models, not internal gear replicas.

The unlock estimate uses spring axial force × an editable friction coefficient × lock radius, divided by the 5:1 ratio and an editable transmission efficiency. It compares demand to an explicitly chosen 50% of the 4.8 V stall figure, not to a manufacturer continuous torque rating. Measure the release torque under load. Electronic payload CG includes battery, measured servo and installed controller stack mass; it excludes structure, wiring and parachute. Set the actual sensor orientation in flight firmware after assembly; the CAD rotation is not a firmware orientation setting.

These configurations complete the mechanical packaging and power connection proposal. They do not implement or qualify autonomous apogee detection, deployment firmware, arming/failsafe behaviour, thermal performance or flight-load capability. The existing position animation remains a kinematic inspection aid.

### Vertical 2S 18650 option

At the D90/86 preset, the ring has a 58.8 mm tooth-tip passage (formerly 49.44 mm), a 60 mm pitch diameter and module 0.6. The 100T ring, four 40T idlers and 20T input retain the 5:1 ratio: 14 degrees of relative ring rotation requires 70 degrees of servo motion. Idler axes move to radius 18 mm; idler tip diameter becomes 25.2 mm and input tip diameter 13.2 mm. The axle seats, support posts and retaining disk follow the enlarged gear layout. Other battery and drive layouts retain their gear sizes. This layout scales the gear diameters with the available ring radius when tube dimensions change.

The nominal radial web between the tooth-root envelope and the retaining-slot envelope is 2.45 mm. This is a geometric clearance, not a verified load capacity. The larger passage is available only after the nose disk and idlers leave; exposed tooth edges can still snag fabric or lines. Check the extraction path with the actual packed parachute or deployment bag and finish the exposed edges without modifying working gear flanks. The live assessment and exported gear metadata include the bore and remaining web. Gear pitch and center distances follow [KHK's gear dimension relationships](https://khkgears.net/gear-knowledge/gear-technical-reference/calculation-gear-dimensions/).

Two opposite cells at Y = ±24 mm share a mass-balanced X offset. Nominal Ø18.6 × 65.2 mm / 46 g per cell are editable installation assumptions for unprotected cells, not a named supplier specification. Four end cups insulate the cells; integral metal collars retain the lower cups. A common 3 mm Al6061 retention plate transfers load into four metal columns through four 8 mm arms and twin 6 mm longitudinal rails. Both cell rings are closed; terminal access bores do not interrupt the rings. Four standard M2 × 8 screws secure the plate. Wire lay-in slots are in the outside plastic cups, not cut through the load-bearing metal rings. A short angled series bridge runs behind the servo at the opposite, servo-disk end. The servo heel has identical open reliefs on both sides and carries the FC on four bonded elastomer dampers, centered on the tube axis. The battery X offset is recalculated to balance the centered FC and offset servo. Two Ø16 mm and one Ø10 mm circular bores lighten the 2 mm web while retaining the case-bearing perimeter, locating rim, four column bosses and controller pads. Two flanged silicone liners provide clear Ø4.8 mm wire passages through Ø6 mm holes behind the servo. Cell retention does not depend on the heel. The removable pack uses formed welded contacts; it is not a loose-cell spring-contact holder. See [2S wiring and service](../../../docs/recovery/battery-2s.md).

The low servo rim locates the heel sideways. Axial case retention still uses the heel cushion, four metal columns, and the front case cushion against the servo disk. This reduces heel-block stock from 26 mm to 6 mm; it is not a verified impact-strength rating. Original four-motor, micro-servo, and ST3215 configurations remain unchanged.

The disk-side ends stand 6 mm away from the servo disk. Its seats have open 10 mm bridge notches. A single PA12 backing with captured end flanges supports the angled B1 bridge behind the servo. The nickel lamella lies on top of its 1 mm substrate in the installed orientation, between low 0.6 mm edge lips. An integral 3 mm high rail in the nose mounting disk supports the full bridge floor; the plastic no longer spans unsupported between its end flanges. The nose-tip insulating cups bear against the closed metal retention rings; contact tabs do not carry cell loads. At the nose-tip end, the two cups incorporate recessed fixed B-/B+ pads and raised polarity marks; their 5.2 mm wire slots route the leads across the outside insulated face, clear of the continuous metal retention rings. Contacts and leads are laid into open seats. The former pods, cassette lids, battery connectors and long return cables are removed. All three electrical nodes remain accessible after removing the nose shell. Cell-specific charge settings, actual service clips, welds, wire strain relief and electrical protection remain installation requirements; see the linked service guide.

### Damped FC mounting for the vertical 2S layout

Four provisional Ø6 × 4 mm silicone/metal isolators replace the thin pads and rigid through-fasteners. Each isolator has a separate lower female M2 insert and upper M2 stud; a 1.1 mm elastomer layer separates their metal ends. A standard M2 × 4 countersunk screw secures only the lower insert to the heel, while the upper stud engages the supplied controller standoff through the shield board. There is no metal bolt spanning the damper. The FC stack stands 4 mm away from the heel. The complete stack moves together; original LiPo layouts retain their existing mounting arrangement.

These are custom bonded isolator envelopes, not a selected catalog product or a verified vibration-isolation rating. Compound, dynamic stiffness, damping, bonding process, allowable deflection and shock retention require selection and testing. Avoid taut wiring that would bypass the compliant mounts. FreeCAD exports the elastomer and both inserts as separate labeled parts for review.

In the damped 2×18650 layout, the complete FC stack is rotated 180° in its mounting plane: the PLS bank faces the positive-X side, away from the offset battery collars. This preserves the reserved plug and wire-bend volume. Configure the actual sensor orientation accordingly.
