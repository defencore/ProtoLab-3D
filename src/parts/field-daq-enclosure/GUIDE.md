# Field DAQ enclosure — Revision 3 (R3)

R3 replaces the earlier enclosure files, which are withdrawn for printing. Use only matching R3 parts from the current bundle and check **REVISION.txt**. This remains a fit-check prototype until physical prints pass the intended rain, wet-ground, snow-melt and handling tests.

This authored enclosure follows the user-supplied adjacent-board arrangement: a 60 × 80 mm KY00012 board for the PSU at the left and a 120 × 80 mm PY board for MCU + ADC at the right. The compact shell is 220 × 116 × 46 mm. Body width W is adjustable from 116 to 180 mm; a four-SP20 narrow-end cassette requires W ≥ 164 mm, giving a 220 × 164 × 46 mm shell in its preset. The upper lid overhangs the core by 1.2 mm on every side: its footprint is 222.4 × (W + 2.4) mm. The installed steel washers and socket screw heads extend the height to 48.3 mm; connector mating envelopes extend beyond the shell footprint. Dimensions are in millimetres. This is a fit-check prototype, not a released environmental enclosure.

## Construction and service

There are 16 printed enclosure occurrences: five PETG parts and eleven TPU parts. The PETG parts are a bottom lid, a 40 mm-high perimeter wall, an upper lid, a removable sensor cassette and one removable central PCB bridge. The TPU parts are two perimeter gaskets, one cassette U-seal, four PCB pads and four under-washer screw seals. The lids are nominally 3 mm thick. The upper lid retains a 3 mm-wide × 5 mm-high underside box beam at Z38–43, local reinforcement ribs to the upper lid. The 1.2 mm overhang sheds runoff outside the core seam. Rounded corners reduce exposed sharp edges and shallow reinforcement controls large flat surfaces. A 0.6 mm-wide engraved outline marks a flat 96 × 44 mm label zone; the complete zone is not recessed, so the lid can print exterior-face down. There are no ventilation, display, switch or indication openings.

The board undersides sit at Z8. At the default 1.6 mm substrate thickness, a component extending 25 mm above the board reaches Z34.6, below the nominal Z43 lid underside. Connector tails and wiring need separate space: do not treat this height allowance as available everywhere.

Each board enters elevated triangular envelopes at its outside edge. The 1.8 mm outer envelope wall and 10.5 mm triangular tip give approximately 8.7 mm of edge engagement. The inner edges now sit in **positive central grooves**, formed by the base support ledges below and the removable bridge roofs above. These are mechanical captures; neither upper-lid pressure nor pad friction is needed to keep the inner edges in the grooves. The user-confirmed clear contact patches are 4 × 10 mm on each inner edge at Y±24. Four 2.8 × 6 × 1.2 mm TPU pads sit within those patches, targeting 20% compression to reduce rattle and local shock without being the retention mechanism.

Four **open triangular inner-corner pockets** locate the PCB corners beside the key at Y±40. Each pocket starts from a floor-connected triangular body with a 10.5 mm X run on the PCB side, a 10.5 mm inward Y reach and a 6 mm outboard Y breadth. The substrate/service cavity is removed from Z8 upward through the full X extent, leaving a bottom seating ledge and a broad outboard body at the selected per-side `fitClearance`. This replaces the earlier narrow fin with a wider load path into the base. The pockets have no upper roof or X-facing wall. They locally limit lateral movement and yaw near the inner edge while leaving the original key release and 9.5 mm board withdrawal route open. All added geometry stays on the PCB side of the central gap, clear of the key spine. The original outer triangular envelopes and key geometry are unchanged. The pocket top extends 2 mm above the board plus fit clearance; inspect real component and solder keepouts over the larger inner-corner lands.

The inner-corner change addresses local lateral play and the fragile load path of the earlier thin guides. Its ideal result is positive location from the existing base material with no extra installed part. The contradiction is that adding inner restraints can also obstruct key release or board removal, while simply thickening them toward the PCB consumes installation clearance. Functional separation assigns Y location to the broad outboard pocket body, X/top capture to the existing key, and the service path to the open X/Z directions. Copying the outer roofed pockets to the inner corners would block the existing withdrawal/lift route; placing Y stops on the sliding key could obstruct its 3 mm release stroke. Open triangular bodies with a through service cavity retain a broad base connection without enclosing the board above or in X. No new supplier geometry or load rating is inferred.

Acceptance checks for this change use local 3 × 3 mm inner-corner substrate witnesses so the outer pockets cannot conceal a missing inner stop: neutral placement must fit, and a Y shift greater than the selected side clearance must encounter the pocket wall. The existing 3 mm key motion, lifted-key exit, 9.5 mm board withdrawal and subsequent lift must remain clear at the minimum/maximum supported PCB thickness and fit clearance. The pockets are integral base features, so the 16 printed enclosure occurrences and installed element references remain unchanged; the changed base geometry receives its corresponding manufactured-detail configuration identity. Geometry checks do not qualify physical print fit, corner solder clearance, clamp loads or board bending.

Install the boards before the bridge so opposed fixed grooves do not block insertion:

1. Insert each board into its outer corner envelopes and lower its inner edge onto the base ledges. Use only the clear substrate lands for contact.
2. Hold the release leaf **0.9 mm up** while lowering the bridge into the entry position and sliding it **+Y by 3 mm**. Then release the leaf into its catch. The nose has no automatic lead-in ramp; do not force the bridge through the catch. The leaf is unloaded in the locked position. The two bridge stations close the central grooves over both boards.
3. Check that the bridge hooks are fully engaged, then fit the lid. Its rear post positively blocks the reverse bridge stroke as a backup even if the small leaf is damaged. The enclosure still uses only the original four M3 closure screws.

For service, remove the lid while leaving the bridge and its four pads installed. Lift the release leaf **0.9 mm**, using a fingernail or the same 2.5 mm Allen key used for the lid screws. The release catch lies outside the PCB outlines, beyond Y+40. Slide the bridge **−Y by 3 mm**, lift it out, then slide one board **9.5 mm toward the central gap** and lift it clear. Remove one board at a time. The **Open lid** view keeps the bridge locked; **PCB service** shows access after release; **Central PCB bridge** isolates its printable part.

The CAD assembly/service route uses bare PCB substrate envelopes. Actual protruding solder joints, leads and wires can catch the envelopes or raised base features during the sliding motion. Inspect and physically test the populated boards, trimming protruding leads where appropriate; do not assume a universal lead-clearance allowance or force a board through the path. The nominal `boardThickness` refers to bare substrate.

The connectors face opposite **narrow ends**. SP16 is centred at X−110, Y0, Z25 with a nominal 3 mm-thick mounting land. The SP20 cassette sits at X+110 and its connectors form a row across Y at Z25. The cassette width is W − 16 mm: 100 mm on the compact W116 body and 148 mm on W164. Two SP20 sockets use 36 mm pitch. Three use 32 mm pitch when W < 124 mm and 36 mm at larger widths. Four use 36 mm pitch and require W ≥ 164 mm to clear the 29 mm collars and panel rails.

Cassettes interchange only between bodies of the same width. W116 accepts matching two-, three- and blank cassettes. W164 accepts matching two-, three-, four- and blank cassettes. Increase **Body width** to at least 164 mm before selecting four sockets; the model never changes width automatically. Remove the top lid before sliding out the cassette. The cassette is located by keys and restrained by the lids, so it needs no additional exposed closure screws. Leave service loops in the internal wiring so removal does not load the solder cups.

Four M3 closure screws pull the two lids and wall together. R3 uses **side-loaded M3 nut traps with a 3 mm-thick roof owned by the base**. Slide the four nuts into their pockets before fitting the perimeter wall. The nuts bear upward against these roofs when tightened; they are not dropped into an axially open pocket. The M3 × 25 / M3 × 30 selector changes the matching base nut-seat stack, so a base exported for one length must use that length. The nominal pocket bottoms are Z16.2 for M3 × 30 and Z21.2 for M3 × 25; the loaded nut sits 0.25 mm above the pocket bottom.

Add **four steel M3 flat washers, 3.2 × 7 × 0.5 mm (ISO 7089 / DIN 125)**. Each steel washer sits at Z44.8–45.3, with the screw head bearing at Z45.3. Under each washer, a PETG hard annulus extends from Z44.2 to Z44.8, with radii 2.9–3.6 mm. The separate TPU seal lies inside that annulus. Once the hard seat contacts the washer, structural clamping is carried through the screw, washer, PETG seat and trapped nut roof; the soft seal does not carry the structural preload.

The four closure axes are X±78, Y±(W/2 − 9.4), keeping the towers clear of the PCB envelopes and end connectors. Measure screw length below the head, verify thread engagement and tighten incrementally across opposite corners until the intended hard stops contact. The geometry does not establish a tightening torque. M3 × 50 screws are too long and are not supported. The body remains 46 mm high; the installed heads reach Z48.3.

## Connector measurements

All connector installation references use the **Waterproof connector** library: an SP16-5 panel connector with five female contacts on the X−110 end, an SP20-4 female panel socket at the first sensor position, and SP20-3 female panel sockets at the remaining positions. Matching cable halves are separate library models. No channel function or pinout is assigned by the enclosure.

The [SP16 supplier drawing](https://klmaster.com/files/goods/182/gallery/germetichnyy_razem_sp16_5_pin_1.jpg) defines a Ø16.5 mounting aperture with **two opposing flats, 15.3 mm apart**. The [SP20 supplier drawing](https://klmaster.com/files/ckeditor/580/5238f7e9a0fea65cb4a62e36c2631fb1.jpg) defines a Ø20.5 aperture with **two opposing flats, 19.5 mm apart**. Both mounted profiles are centred at Z25, with the flats above and below the opening. If aperture diameter D is adjusted, the flat-to-flat span remains D − 1.2 mm for SP16 and D − 1 mm for SP20.

The library models reconstruct the supplier dimensions and include the housing, contacts, retaining nut and seal. They are source-dimension CAD, not manufacturer CAD; undimensioned contact positions and small moulded details remain illustrative. Check the delivered parts, including their mating cable halves, against the selected supplier execution.

Before printing the full wall and cassette, record:

- Mounting barrel diameter, flats or other anti-rotation features and recommended clearance.
- Flange diameter, retaining nut dimensions, allowable panel thickness and connector gasket seat.
- Inboard projection of the body and solder cups, insulated joint height and cable bend space.
- Outboard mating-plug envelope and access for tightening, connecting and disconnecting.

Reserve at least a 20 mm inboard band from each X end wall without tall components, then increase it if the actual parts, wire insulation or bend radius require more room. The maximum-height component reference zones are PSU X−84…−38, Y±32 and MCU X−20…84, Y±32; the board outlines remain unchanged. Four connectors at 36 mm pitch must be checked with the delivered flanges, nuts and tooling. Contact counts of three, four or five are electrical choices; each selected library housing retains its own mounting profile.

## Fits and seals

`fitClearance` is a per-side sliding-fit allowance, initially 0.25 mm. Print the **Fit coupon** with the intended PETG and profile. Check edge over-extrusion, the first-layer bulge, PCB entry and removable-cassette keys before choosing the final clearance. Deburr only the intended entry edges; do not indiscriminately sand sealing lands.

Each lid uses a separate **1.35 mm-wide × 1.2 mm-high TPU gasket in a 2.0 mm-wide × 0.96 mm-deep gland**. R3 targets 20% thickness compression and approximately 84.4% gland volume fill, leaving room for lateral deformation. This target must be qualified with the actual filament hardness and print. A separate U-shaped TPU seal lies behind the cassette sides and bottom. Its free upper ends reach Z43.24 to provide preload at the upper lid junction. Cassette stop faces remove the earlier 0.25 mm outward float, and an integrated continuous bottom compression rib plus matching top back rail and lid brace limit inward travel to 0.1 mm at both ends of the cassette. The nominal U-seal compression target is 20–26.7%. Its bottom channel is 2.6 mm high with a 1.1–1.2 mm installed gap, targeting approximately 77–84% nominal volume fill. These ribs and rails are integral to the existing parts and add no printed occurrences. Four separate under-washer TPU seals have 4.84 mm free OD, 2.8 mm free ID and 0.8 mm free thickness. They compress to 0.6 mm under the steel washers, with approximately 84.3% nominal available-volume fill after stretching over the 3 mm screw shaft. The 2.8 mm free bore provides 0.1 mm radial interference. Actual thread roughness and wet sealing still require physical tests. The interchangeable cassette adds a sealing interface; inspect the U-seal junctions with both lid seals carefully. Check contact around the entire long perimeter because four corner fasteners do not guarantee even compression at the midpoint of each side.

Keep the four PCB pads in their intended bridge pockets with a small amount of PETG/TPU-compatible adhesive if needed for assembly. Gaskets use their contact fit for positioning; if a thin adhesive tack is needed, keep it outside the active sealing face and do not add a thick bond line to the compression stack. Select and qualify the adhesive with the actual PETG, TPU, wet conditions and service temperature.

The model has no assigned IP or impact rating. FDM walls, connector seats, a lid gasket and a cassette seal all need practical verification. Test with an empty enclosure first. Check for pinholes, incomplete extrusion and warped mating lands; perform the intended spray or dust exposure and inspect inside before installing electronics. A printed TPU ring is not automatically equivalent to a moulded elastomer seal.

## Suggested print sequence

Use **Print layout** to inspect intended orientations, then put the separate items on suitable build plates. The complete inspection layout is not a claim that every part fits one printer plate. Export **Bottom lid**, **Perimeter wall**, **Top lid**, **Sensor cassette**, **Central PCB bridge**, **TPU gasket**, **Fit coupon** and **Central groove test base** individually when preparing a print. PCB and connector references are excluded from physical exports.

The prepared print bundle contains an independent, bed-normalized STL for each of the 16 printed occurrences, plus two small test coupons and the matching cassette variants prepared for that body width. The compact bundle contains 20 STL files and the wide bundle 21; the test coupons are not extra assembly occurrences. The compact bundle must not include a four-hole cassette that cannot fit its shell. Repeated pad and screw-seal filenames represent separate occurrences. Choose one sensor cassette for an assembled enclosure. The assembly preview STL is for inspection and must not be printed as one object.

1. Print the small fit coupon and check the delivered PCB and connector barrels against it. The coupon includes one envelope socket, the SP16 / SP20 apertures with both opposing flats, and a side-loaded M3 nut trap at X26, Y−8. Use the trap to check sideways nut insertion, roof capture and the start of the thread. Do not tighten an M3 × 30 screw fully against this coupon: its screw-tip well is blind. Check closure seating and tightening only in the complete enclosure stack.
2. Print the **34 × 104 mm Central groove test base** and the production central bridge before the full 220 mm base. The small test base has the same bayonet plinths, catch, four edge ledges and four open triangular inner-corner pockets. Its floor spans X−46…−12 and Y−48…56 to support the full pocket footprint; it replaces the narrower guide coupon. Use bare strips of the actual PCB substrate to test local lateral centering, positive groove capture, the +Y3 mm lock, 0.9 mm leaf release and −Y3 mm unlocking stroke. Reuse the production bridge in the enclosure; the test base is not an assembly part. Only after this sample works should you print the full base and check the complete service path with the actual populated boards, including solder clearance.
3. Print the wall and selected cassette; fit the actual connectors and inspect nut/tool access and wiring clearance. Keep the bridge latch and tool access free of wires.
4. Print the lid. Dry-assemble all PETG parts at the hard stops and verify the screw stack before adding seals.
5. Print TPU gaskets and pads separately. Fit the four required steel washers and the smaller seals underneath, then verify hard-stop contact, gasket contact and board preload before testing the empty enclosure.

For a Bambu P2S with a 0.4 mm nozzle, a 0.20 mm layer, five wall loops and six top/bottom layers are sensible PETG starting settings, not qualified process limits. Use the filament maker's temperature and drying recommendations, a calibrated first layer and a moderate outer-wall speed. Start with approximately 25–35% infill where there is a meaningful internal volume; the wall loops carry the thin shell. Inspect bridging and local overhangs before accepting a support-free slice. Preserve sealing faces when deciding whether a small local support is needed.

Print lids with their broad exterior faces on the plate and their internal supports upward. Print the central bridge **groove-roof down**, with its broad roof faces toward the bed; check its hooks, leaf and tool window in the slicer. Print the wall standing on its lower perimeter. Print the cassette with its broad exterior face down and the aperture axes vertical. Print the TPU seals flat, with continuous perimeters and a fully filled cross-section. Keep TPU details separate from PETG rather than relying on a fused multi-material seal. Ordinary flexible TPU should use the external spool path unless its manufacturer explicitly declares AMS compatibility; Bambu's AMS 2 Pro list specifies **TPU for AMS**.

For the compact **W116** body, three proposed build plates produce grey lids, a black wall and cassette, and black soft details without filament colour changes:

1. **Grey PETG — bottom and top lids.** Place the 222.4 × 118.4 mm upper lid and 220 × 116 mm bottom lid alongside one another with an 8 mm gap. Their combined nominal bounding rectangle is 222.4 × 242.4 mm, within a 256 × 256 mm plate.
2. **Black PETG — wall and sensor cassette.** Centre the 220 × 116 mm perimeter wall on the plate. Place the approximately 104 × 36 mm compact cassette flat inside the empty wall cavity, centred within it as a separate object. Place the central bridge as another separate object inside the remaining empty cavity where it clears the cassette and wall; these three parts use black PETG.
3. **Black TPU — all soft details.** Place the two 217.35 × 113.35 mm perimeter gaskets alongside one another with an 8 mm gap, giving a 217.35 × 234.7 mm nominal bounding rectangle. Place the U-seal, four PCB pads and four under-washer seals within the empty gasket interiors, each as a separate object.

For the wide **W164** body, plan approximately five plates: one grey PETG plate for the 222.4 × 166.4 mm upper lid and one for the 220 × 164 mm bottom lid, one black PETG plate for the wall with its approximately 152 × 36 mm cassette nested in the empty cavity, and the separate central bridge wherever it clears the wall and cassette, followed by one black TPU plate for each 217.35 × 161.35 mm perimeter gasket. Nest the U-seal, pads and screw seals in the empty gasket interiors as separate objects where they fit. Two wide covers or two wide gaskets do not fit the compact side-by-side arrangement.

These are bounding-box packing proposals, not a final toolpath. Check object collisions, actual print profiles, excluded plate areas, brims, skirts, supports and nozzle travel in the slicer before accepting each arrangement. Keep every part separate; do not merge the cassette or bridge with the wall or the small TPU details with a gasket. The existing AMS can hold both PETG colours, but these single-colour plates avoid colour changes and their purge waste. Use the external spool path for ordinary flexible TPU.

Grey lids can reduce solar heat absorption compared with black lids, but actual internal temperature still needs measurement in the intended sun, enclosure orientation and PSU load.

## TRIZ design decisions

The design uses the [TRIZ inventive principles](https://triz.org/principles/) to resolve specific construction contradictions:

| Contradiction                                          | Principles applied               | Construction decision                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rigid PCB capture versus removable boards              | Segmentation; preliminary action | Insert boards first, then install one removable bridge that closes the inner grooves.                                  |
| No additional bolts versus a serviceable FDM mechanism | Intermediary; dynamics           | Bridge hooks lock with a 3 mm stroke and an unloaded release leaf; the existing lid supplies a positive backup stop.   |
| Impact restraint versus PCB stress                     | Local quality                    | Rigid groove walls limit displacement at clear edge patches; soft pads reduce rattle rather than supplying retention.  |
| Sealing versus a replaceable connector panel           | Segmentation                     | The internal bridge and external cassette are independent; board service adds no penetration through the sealing wall. |

This is a design method and geometric construction, not an IP, drop-life or material-fatigue qualification. The leaf and release motion require a physical fit and cycle test with the printed PETG.

## Identification and field qualification

Use one weather-resistant printed label inside the engraved lid outline for the actual channel names and pinout. Suggested information is **PRESSURE**, **LOAD**, and **RS485 / 14–20 V**, with channel numbers, polarity and a serial number. Electrical assignments come from the finished circuit; the enclosure does not assign a pinout. Keep connector numbering aligned with the label when changing cassette variants.

The intended exposure is **wet ground, dirt, rain, snow and meltwater**. Test the empty, fully assembled print with the real connectors mated or covered by their matching sealed caps. The library omits the protective caps, so inspect those real interfaces separately. Soil or grit on a gasket can create a leak path: clean the contact surfaces before closure and inspect them after field handling.

Use representative rain and meltwater exposure, cold handling and realistic drops, then inspect inside for moisture and check the nut traps, hard seats, cassette stops and board retention. Include wet/cold cycles and repeated opening. Printed PETG and TPU, gasket hardness, layer bonding and the actual connector seals determine performance. Check sustained PSU temperature separately. Keep electronics out until physical tests pass. R3 has no assigned IP or impact rating.
